Local video player for Android: your own files, the phone's hardware decoders first with bundled FFmpeg decoders as the fallback, and phone-to-phone transfer over your own Wi-Fi. No account, no cloud.

## What's new

- **Several videos at once** — a long press in the library starts a selection, which is the one thing the library could not do before: every action took one file. The bar counts what is in it ("3 selected") and takes the details of the whole selection, sends it to another phone, or deletes it. Sending fills the Transfer tab's queue rather than starting three jobs from three taps, so the receiving phone sees one transfer. Deleting names how many files are about to go before it asks, and reports how many went instead of showing one notice per file.
- **A sleep timer** — the player's title bar has a timer that stops playback after 10, 20, 30 or 60 minutes, or when the video playing now ends. It says which of those is set, and says so when it has fired. Nothing is left armed for the next video: a stop that is still waiting when the player closes is not carried over.
- **What the file actually is** — the details sheet reads the container instead of the file name: the video and audio codec, the bitrate, the frame rate, and how many video, audio and subtitle tracks there are. It reads off the main thread and says "reading the file…" while it works, so a large file or a slow card cannot freeze the sheet.
- **Favourites** — a video can be starred from its details, the library has a "Favourites" chip beside the folder chips that counts what is in it, and that view opens with a Play all. The list is kept on the device, so it survives a restart, and it never leaves the phone.
- **The control bar sits where you want it** — the arrow in the player's title bar used to flip the bar between the top and the bottom; it now offers top, bottom, left and right, and Settings → Playback carries the same four chips. Along a side edge the seek bar stands upright (it fills upwards, like a volume slider) and the buttons form a narrow grid beside it, because a phone held in landscape is not tall enough for one row of ten buttons. Whatever was chosen before the update is kept: a bar that sat at the top is still at the top.
- **The new screens speak** — the selection bar, the details sheet, the sleep-timer choices, the favourites chip and the four-position picker all carry spoken labels, and a recent file's row says what tapping it does ("Resume Movie") rather than reading the title alone.
- **Unchanged** — 1.5.5 upgrades over 1.5.4 in place, signed with the same key, and the bundled decoders, the transfer protocol and the stored settings are untouched apart from the control-bar position, which is carried over from the old switch.

156 JVM tests pass, and the emulator job runs 40 instrumented tests, including the new cases for the four control-bar edges. Full changelog: https://github.com/NarDecH/Android_VDO_Player/blob/main/docs/CHANGELOG.md

## Before you install

Minimum Android 8.0 (API 26). `INTERNET` is declared only so the Transfer tab can open local sockets — nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and a log leaves the device only when you tap Share or Save.
